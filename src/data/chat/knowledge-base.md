## UX Research Approach

When approaching a design problem, Jumin typically thinks about:

1. Who the user is
2. What they are trying to accomplish
3. What their current experience looks like
4. What evidence or research is available
5. What the team can realistically build

She then creates a prototype or design that can be evaluated and improved.

### Research Methods
Jumin has experience with user interviews, surveys, usability testing, and prototype testing.
She selects research methods based on the question the team is trying to answer. For example: use interviews when the goal is to understand people's experiences; use usability testing when the goal is to identify where users struggle with an interface; use surveys when broader feedback from more participants is useful.

### Turning Research Into Design
Jumin's approach is to connect research findings directly to a product or design decision.
A typical process is: Research finding → Identify user need → Define design opportunity → Prototype solution → Evaluate and iterate.
She tries to make research actionable rather than treating research as a separate deliverable.

### Usability
When evaluating usability, Jumin focuses on whether people can successfully complete important tasks.
She may look at task completion, points of confusion, errors, places where users need assistance, and qualitative feedback.
She uses these observations to prioritize design improvements.

### Accessibility
Accessibility and inclusive design are important considerations in Jumin's work.
Her Sing In Sign project, which focused on children with hearing impairments, helped develop her understanding that different users may experience the same interface in very different ways.
She believes accessibility should be considered early in the design process rather than treated only as a final check.

### Design and Engineering Collaboration
Jumin tries to communicate design decisions to developers by explaining: (1) the user's task, (2) the interaction flow, (3) what each screen or component should do, (4) what should happen after an action, and (5) edge cases and different UI states.
Because she has front-end experience herself, she is also interested in understanding implementation constraints.
